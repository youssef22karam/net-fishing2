# Netfishing — cozy deck net-fishing (React + Vite + Tailwind + three.js)

## Split-file edition (every file ≤ 30,000 bytes)

For GitHub upload / Arena: no file in this repo exceeds 30,000 bytes.

- `src/engine/boat.js` (127,856 bytes) is split into `src/engine/parts/boat.part01–05.js`
- `src/engine/sea.js` (80,243 bytes) is split into `src/engine/parts/sea.part01–03.js`
- Split is at line boundaries; parts are raw slices with nothing added.
- `tools/reassemble.mjs` concatenates the parts byte-for-byte back into
  `src/engine/boat.js` and `src/engine/sea.js` (both git-ignored), and refuses
  any part over 30,000 bytes. It runs automatically in `npm run dev`,
  `npm run build` and `npm run preview` — just run `npm install` first, then
  the usual commands. To reassemble by hand: `npm run reassemble`.
- `package-lock.json` (86,776 bytes) is omitted; it is regenerated by `npm install`.

**Arena instructions:** pull the whole repo, run `npm install`, then
`npm run build` — reassembly happens automatically before Vite runs. Do not
edit the generated `src/engine/boat.js` / `sea.js` directly; edit the parts
(or reassemble, edit, and re-split at line boundaries under 30,000 bytes).

Built on top of `youssef22karam/netfishing` (its FFT sea, wake sim, weather, buoyant lobster boat and cameras are kept unchanged and live in `src/engine/`). The old fishing game and its mini-game were deleted. All gameplay is new, in `src/game/` and `src/games/`.

**Controls:** `B` stand on deck · `V` Auto-Creep · hold `F` (or click with the pointer locked) and release in the green to throw · `G` haul · `N` next net · `T` mesh · `H` sell · `Z` workshop · `C` camera · `W/S/A/D` drive · `Q/E` trawl warp · `K` help.

**Visibility** (`FishWorld.visibility`) is the product of these terms, with no on/off switch:
- water: `exp(-k·depth·slant·.5)`, where `k = lerp(1.2, .2, clarity)`
- haze with distance, plus storm
- light level and sun elevation
- sun glare when looking toward the sun
- sea state: `1/(1+.12·Hs^1.5)`

The result drives the dark-patch alpha and each fish's alpha.

## Net generator (`netgen.js`)

```
q      = max(0, ln(price/pMin) / ln(pMax/pMin))          bands: cast 15–3000, trawl 400–60k, seine 800–120k, gill 60–30k
g(q)   = q (q<=1) | 1 + 0.8·ln(1 + 4(q-1)) (q>1)          soft growth, finite for any price (clamped at 1e15)
dim    = a·(b/a)^g · jitter(hash(type:price))             same price = same net
cast:   radius = clamp(dim(2,5),2,5) · weight = dim(1.3,8.5 kg)(+growth) · sink = .7·sqrt(.72W/(.12R²+.5)) · load = .05W kN
trawl:  mouth = dim(4,40) m · load = .45·w·h^.7 | seine: L = dim(40,400) · load = .02·L·D^.6 | gill: L = dim(30,300) · load = .006·L·D
```

Material tier is one of 6 (cotton → aramid-Dyneema), chosen by `q`. Durability, twine, rope, float count, sinker count and the 3 mesh options also come from `q` and the hash. Sinker and float instances are capped at 72 and 64.

## Boat ↔ net compatibility

`over = net.load / boat.cap[type]`. Mismatches change performance first and show a warning chip second. Nothing is ever blocked.

- `speedMul = clamp(1.08 − .5·max(0, over−.6), .1, 1)` scales boat speed while towing.
- `openMul = clamp(1.25 − .62·over, .22, 1)` limits how far the trawl mouth or cast-net spread opens.
- `strain` raises engine noise (more fish spooked) and winch and cord tension in the 2D games.

| Boat | Pull kN | Cast cap | Trawl cap | Seine cap | Gill cap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Open skiff | 1.8 | .28 | 1.6 | 1.3 | .9 |
| Lobster boat (the base 3D hull) | 9 | .5 | 7 | 5.5 | 3.5 |
| Stern trawler | 34 | .8 | 26 | 20 | 12 |

Price ranges (ideal ≤ / max ≈) per boat are computed live in the Workshop by bisection. For example, the cast net ideal limit is $225 on the skiff, $1,252 on the lobster boat and $5,794 on the trawler. All three boats use the same 3D hull; they differ in engine and gear specs only.

## The four 2D games

- **Cast net — "Race the Rim".** A cross-section of the sea under your landed net, built from the fish actually inside the footprint.
  - Mechanic: the net sinks as a bell while fish panic after a delay and bolt outward and down. Hold Space to cinch the cord (the rim contracts and lifts). Tap the water to herd stragglers inward, which also spooks them.
  - Strategy: cinch early for a safe small catch of the shallow fish, or wait for the rim to pass a deeper layer for a bigger but riskier one.
  - Hard parts: the fish dive faster than a light net sinks, cord tension rises with load (and sheds fish at the limit), fine mesh sinks badly, coarse mesh lets small fish through, and waves and current push the net.
  - Perfect: 80% or more of the reachable, mesh-retained fish with no emergency close.
- **Trawl — "Hold the Lane".** A side-scrolling depth-keeping game.
  - Mechanic: steer a lagging winch so the mouth sits in the depth lane where fish pass.
  - Strategy: Space surges (wider mouth, faster tow) but loads the winch, and low nets risk rock snags.
  - Hard parts: an oversized net on a weak boat has less opening, more winch lag and faster tension build-up, and a winch overload ends the haul early.
  - Perfect: 85% or more of the retained fish, no snags and no overload.
- **Purse seine — "Pursing Rhythm".** A two-lane rhythm game.
  - Mechanic: each on-beat pull tightens the ring and shrinks the leak gap under it.
  - Strategy: steady tempo gives wider timing windows, fast tempo closes quicker but with narrow windows.
  - Hard parts: misses slacken the line and open the gap, a weak winch (high `over`) makes each pull smaller, and swell makes the lanes sway.
  - Perfect: 82% or better accuracy with leakage of 10% or less.
- **Gillnet — "Pick the Net".** A sorting conveyor.
  - Mechanic: the net rolls toward the drum; click to free tangled fish (1–3 clicks each, more when the mesh matches the fish).
  - Strategy: keep or release (right-click or Shift-click for undersize fish), and free the red-ringed bycatch first.
  - Hard parts: the conveyor speeds up and sea state jostles the items.
  - Perfect: no bycatch harmed, no wrong keeps and at most one keeper lost.

Estimates are shown before committing: at the cast game's top bar, and for trawl, seine and gill as "Estimated haul ≈ N fish · $X · difficulty %" before `G`.
